A Kinematic Framework for
Extensible Continuum Robots with Bellow Backbone and Follow-the-Leader Motion

Abstract

Extensible continuum robots introduce controllable length deployment in addition to bending. However, many existing designs achieve ``extension'' by adding a separate insertion mechanism to a conventional continuum robot, complicating mechanism design and reducing motion dexterity. This paper presents an extensible rod-driven continuum robot with n segments and a bellows backbone, in which 3n rod lengths jointly encode bending, bending direction, extension, and retraction. An extensible piecewise constant curvature (ePCC) model is proposed, mapping these joint inputs directly to the task space positions. Our theoretical analysis shows that controllable continuum robot extension significantly enlarges the workspace and enhances local manipulability compared with the fixed‑length counterpart. An online Jacobian adaptive controller is further developed for extension-aware tip tracking and follow-the-leader motion to maximize the advantages of extensible continuum robots. Extensive experimental results are presented to verify the kinematics and controller. This work establishes a feasible pathway for continuum robots to gain an additional geometric degree of freedom through controllable length variation, modeled and controlled by an augmented kinematic framework without extra actuators.
